Long-bond rate — your real-rate gauge 10 / 20 / 30 yr

Two different things, both here on purpose. The long-bond nominal yield above is what you watch as the market's price of money. Textbook "real yield" is that number minus inflation — plotted in the next panel — and the market's own real-rate estimate is the TIPS yield (enter it as a note if you track it). Keeping both lets you compare your read against the standard one without trusting anyone's press release.

About SomiFinance

A single-file personal finance dashboard — net worth, budgeting, macro signals and an economic calendar in one page. There's no account, no server and no build step: everything runs in the browser and your figures are saved only to this browser's localStorage under the key somifinance.v1. Nothing is transmitted anywhere.

Because the data lives in one browser profile, Export is your only backup — use it regularly, and Import to restore or move to another machine.

The charting library is bundled into this file, so by default, opening the page makes no network requests at all — it works fully offline. Three things can reach out, each only when you ask them to: the TradingView calendar widget loads the first time you open the Economic Calendar tab, the Treasury and BLS feeds are fetched when you press Refresh on Macro Signals, and an exchange-rate lookup runs once a day if you pick a non-USD currency. None of them are sent your financial data.

One caveat worth knowing: the calendar widget is third-party code from TradingView. It runs inside a sandboxed frame with no access to this page or to your saved data, and TradingView sees only that someone requested the widget — but opening that tab does contact their servers.

If you'd rather macro data updated by itself, Settings ⚙ → Auto-refresh can fetch it once a day (When stale) or on every reload (Every load). It ships Off, so the no-requests-on-load behaviour above holds until you opt in.
